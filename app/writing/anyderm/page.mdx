# AnyDerm Whitepaper

**Roshan Kern**\
**September 1, 2026**


## Overview

5 years ago I went through the most horrific experience of any new college student hoping to start a relationship - acne.
All jokes aside, this was one of the hardest challenges of my life.
I could rationally understand that acne should, in theory at least, not prevent me from finding "the one".
But emotionally, I was so distraught that I would wear my COVID face mask long after I needed it just to cover as much of my face as possible.
During the worst of it, I did my best to avoid mirrors, windows, etc for fear of seeing my skin and once again feeling ashamed.
Brainrotting [r/acne](https://www.reddit.com/r/acne/) became a past time for me as I searched for others that could share my pain and possibly even escaped it.

Unfortunately, my self-hatred of my face was so intense that I made sure any image that made it into my camera roll was too low quality to see my face in high detail.
Now, I can't provide any evidence of just how bad it was.
I even made an Instagram account sepcifically for tracking my face in a way I could not easily open, but that account is long gone.

Everything worked out fine of course, but it felt like it took forever to get that way.
Each dermatology visit was particularly painful. 
The doctor would glance at my skin (usually over my webcam), hear what I remembered happened with my acne in the past month, and take 5 seconds to figure out how to proceed.

I remember being extremely frustrated because I knew accutane works for nearly everyone that tries it.
I would of course been fine with something less extreme.
In fact, anything that worked would have been fine.
But these doctors took less than 15 minutes to casually evaluate my face, give a simple recommendation, and move on.
It didn't feel like they took my acne seriously, even though there was nothing more important in my life during this time.

As diagnostics and the cost of intelligence approach zero, I find myself wondering what the future looks like for others with skin conditions.
New devices like the [Lumeria Lumoscope](https://lumeria.skin/buy) and [OpenDerm](https://openderm.github.io) have validated that others are wondering as well.

In this whitepaper, I outline my aim to build the highest information:cost system in dermatology.
I hypothesize that for under $500, I can build a hardware + software system that captures enough information to make valuable clinical decisions in dermatology.
In particular, I hope to utilize consumer smartphone cameras, open-source robotics, and AI-driven reasoning to inform acne patients about the best way to address their condition.
Macro and micro scans would enable measurement of inflammatory lesion count and size, the erythema around each lesion, the fraction of follicles that are plugged, porphyrin fluorescence from *C. acnes*, and the post-inflammatory marks left behind as lesions heal.
Tracked across the same follicles week after week, those features become a hyper-personalized acne diagnosis, treatment plan, and intervention tracker.

## Problem

My dermatologists were not being lazy.
They were making a decision with almost no information.
The only information in the webcam meetings we had was my grainy face and hazy recollection of my skin that month.
Real longitudinal dermatology requires consistent imaging of the face with high detail and [specialized light](citations for this).
The worse the quality of the data, the longer it takes to make decisions about if something is working.
This is why I had to wait up to 2 months to try a new intervention when only using my webcam and memory.

Systems already exist to get higher skin quality information.
They trade off time, cost, and accuracy.

<ApproachesFigure />

Wide-field scanners resolve the whole face in one exposure, but spread their pixels across all of it, so a single follicle is never resolved.
Robotic scanners get close enough to resolve one, but the ones that exist cost thousands of dollars and live in clinics.
Smartphone imaging is the only tier you would actually use every week, and it is the tier with nothing holding the camera still.

Nothing currently exists to get high quality, cheap, longitudinal data for acne.
This is the gap that AnyDerm is designed to fill.

## System Overview

### Design

What does it take to photograph the same square millimeter of skin two weeks apart?
You need the camera in the same place, pointed the same way, at the same distance, lit the same way.
And you need to know which square millimeter you got.
AnyDerm is a 3D printed robot arm that carries a phone and a small light around a face.
It images in 3 different modailites off one head.

| Mode | How | What it sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RGB | White LED ring | Lesions, pigmentation, texture, color |
| Cross-polarized | Polarizer on the LEDs, analyzer rotated 90 degrees at the lens | Erythema and inflammation, with the surface glare removed |
| UVA fluorescence | 405 nm ring | Porphyrins produced by *C. acnes*, as a proxy for follicular activity |

A one minute coarse pass covers the whole face for global metrics and registration.
Then, high-magnification scanning covers the regions worth following follicle by follicle.

### Data Processing

We can extract the following dermatology features from each scan.

| Feature | Extracted from | Why it matters for acne |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inflammatory lesion count and area | RGB and cross-polarized, per map region | The endpoint essentially every acne trial is scored on |
| Perilesional erythema | Cross-polarized redness in a ring around each lesion | Inflammation moves well before lesion counts do |
| Follicular plugging fraction | Ostium area and optical density, per follicle | Every follicle becomes its own before-and-after, instead of a number in a total |
| Porphyrin density | 405 nm fluorescence, per follicle | A proxy for *C. acnes* activity, and the fastest moving signal on the head |
| Sebum and gloss | Unpolarized minus cross-polarized | Surface oil, without paying for a second polarizer channel |
| Post-inflammatory marks | Cross-polarized, matched to a lesion that has since resolved | The part of "clear skin" that outlasts the lesion by months |

After multiple scans from an acne patient, we can create an intra-patient and inter-patient dataset to determine how to best help everyone.

### Summary
**Total Cost:** $355\
**Manipulation:** six degrees of freedom, a ~40 cm reach envelope, and a 5 cm working distance, with about 0.43° of backlash in the joints.\
**Imaging:** three imaging modalities, a whole-face coarse pass at around 100 µm per pixel in about a minute, and targeted high-magnification tiles at 5–8 µm per pixel.

## Estimated Performance

Performance estimations are ultimately just that, an estimate.
I intend to push the system as far as it can go.
But, here is the napkin math for what this system should be able to achieve.

Inflammatory lesions typically present with 30-80 leisons over the course of a clinical trial.
This means we need to observe ~28 lesions changing to be "significant", which takes months.

With thousands of follicles observed, we can measure a significant change with only 1-5% of these follicles changing.
Because follicles also develop faster than lesions, our total measurement time goes to weeks.

How fast we can measure changes is still limited by biology though.

| Signal | Channel | Moves in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Porphyrin fluorescence | 405 nm | 3–7 days |
| Perilesional erythema | Cross-polarized | 1–2 weeks |
| Follicular plugging fraction | High-magnification RGB and cross-pol | ~3 weeks |
| Inflammatory lesion count | RGB | 8–12 weeks |
| Texture and scarring | High-magnification, all channels | 24+ weeks |

A realistic estimate for how fast we can notice skin health changing is 3 weeks.
Note that for many acne treatments, skin can actually get worse before it gets better which will further delay signifcant results.

Repeat scan above multiple times and see how well we can measure changes.


## Risks

**Autofocus wrecks the scale.**
This is the one that worries me most.
OpenDerm's own documentation states that autofocus changes magnification about 1% per frame at macro distance, because focus position *is* effective focal length, and their operator checklist locks manual focus and turns image stabilization off.
A phone does neither by default.
1% of scale error is larger than most of the effects in the table above, so if iOS focus lock does not genuinely freeze the lens element, every area measurement is fiction.
The reference patch in frame gives a per-frame scale check, which turns this from silent corruption into a rejected frame.
Sensor-shift stabilization is the harder half — it cannot be disabled through the public API for stills, and it moves the principal point between frames.
If both prove unfixable, a fixed-focus dedicated camera comes back onto the bill of materials and takes the cost argument with it.

**Skin moves.**
Deformation sits at the top of the error budget and there is no fix for it, only mitigation.
Expression, hydration, time of day, and what side you slept on all shift a follicle relative to its neighbors without anything biological happening.
Local registration rather than global, and measuring in each scan's own unwarped frame, keeps this from turning into fake change — but it puts a floor under *f*, and I do not know where that floor is.

**Motion blur at the high-magnification tier.**
At 5 µm per pixel, 200 µm of movement during an exposure is 40 pixels of smear and the follicle is gone.
Sweeping continuously is fine for the coarse pass and probably not fine here.
Stop-and-shoot fixes it and costs scan time, and scan time is the compliance constraint that the whole protocol is built around.

**The UVA channel may not survive daylight.**
Porphyrin fluorescence is the weakest signal on the head, and going non-contact gives up the exact thing dermatoscopes touch skin for.
The shroud has to be small enough to clear a nose and still block a room.
If it cannot be both, the fastest-moving signal in the whole system only works in a dark room, which is a real constraint on a device meant to live on a desk.

**The registration might not close.**
Nobody has published a repeatability statistic for this class of system — not OpenDerm, not Lumeria, not anyone.
The approach I am building on has never been independently validated, and I would be starting from a pose estimate five to ten times worse than the gantry that has not validated it.
The narrow field of view makes it harder: at 110 mm standoff an 8° field already makes depth and rotation nearly degenerate, and I have not computed what my field is at 50 mm.

**The biology might not cooperate.**
The three-week claim rests on follicular plugging leading visible inflammation reliably, and optics cannot see a closed microcomedone through scattering.
What I would actually measure is a composite proxy — ostium area, optical density, perifollicular erythema, porphyrin signal — not the plug itself.
If that proxy does not track the thing it stands in for, the timeline collapses back to conventional lesion counting and the speed argument goes with it.
This is the risk I can do the least about from the engineering side, and the one most worth checking early.
