# Programming Personality

**Roshan Kern**\

## Introduction

> *Der Mensch kann tun was er will; er kann aber nicht wollen was er will.*
>
> Man can do what he wills but he cannot will what he wills.
>
> — Arthur Schopenhauer, *On the Freedom of the Will* (1839)

Or can he?

Confucius reported otherwise. 
At fifteen he set out to become a certain kind of person. 
By seventy, the gap between what he wanted to do and what he thought he should do had closed.
In other words, his affective state finally matched his reflexive state.
It took fifty-five years of practice, and he died three years later.

In this piece, I look at how people can program their personalities so behaviors that serve their goals feel rewarding, while behaviors that harm their goals feel aversive.
AKA, motivation/discipline on demand. 
AKA, affective state = reflective state.
Instead of acting against our desires more reliably, I want to learn how to most effectively change first-order desires so they match the second-order volitions we already have.

A good way to understand how we might achieve this, and why it would be very useful, is to read about [personality basins](https://near.blog/personality-basins/).
[Near](https://near.blog/) frames personality as a landscape of attractor basins: stable configurations that explain why people persist as they are, and why change is hard.

![A personality basin](/personality-basins.jpg)
*Your personality is the black path updated by years of decisions in your environment.*

Evertime you make a choice, you sink further into a personality basin. 
These can be good (ex: a basin of weightlifting - you have formed an interest and habit in going to the gym) or bad (ex: a basin of brainrotting).
Personality basins usually take years to form and change.
An addiction to alcohol, for example, usually starts as a couple of beers with friends but progresses to a 12 pack while alone.
It will take this addict [years of therapy and struggle](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15797639/) to escape this alcoholic personality basin.

Accelerating this process would help people, and doing it at scale would change the world.
GLP-1 agonists are an example of this happening in real time.
People on them go from constantly thinking about and consuming food to eliminating their food noise practically overnight.
For the first time in over two decades, U.S. obesity has had a [statistically significant decrease in prevalence](https://news.gallup.com/poll/696599/obesity-rate-declining.aspx).

If you are unsure about the utility of changing our desires instead of more effectively resisting them, I highly encourage you to talk to someone who has struggled with obesity for years before trying a GLP-1 agonist.
Like all technology, this has the potential for major abuse.
But for the sake of this piece, I will only focus on how to most effectively program personality at scale.
I will leave the ethical discussion of when this makes sense to use for another article.

## Success Criteria

What does success look like for helping people at scale?
I spent some time thinking about what I as a user would care about when trying one of these methods.
The 12 criteria I came up with group nicely into 3 categories.

**Efficacy: Does it work, on whom, and does it keep working.**
- Magnitude: How large a change the intervention produces in the intended mental state.
- Steerability: How selectively the effect hits the intended process rather than everything else.
- Reliability: What fraction of users get the intended effect rather than nothing or the opposite.
- Durability: How long the effect lasts after a single treatment.
- Tolerance: Whether the effect holds up across repeated use.

**Tolerability: What it costs the user to accept and sustain.**
- Safety: The probability and severity of harm from normal use.
- Invasiveness: How much the intervention physically enters or disrupts the body.
- Reversibility: How quickly an unwanted effect can be stopped or undone.
- Burden: How much time, effort, and repetition the user must contribute.
- Speed: How long from administration to peak effect.

**Deployability: Whether you can legally and economically ship it.**
- Regulation: How difficult it is to legally sell to a healthy adult for enhancement.
- Cost: Raw materials and production cost per user, absent regulatory overhead.

I tried to keep this list as concise possible.
These are all relevant to the success of helping people program their personalities at scale.

Now, let's see what options exist to optimize for all of these criteria.

## Methods

What methods or technologies will facilitate programming personalities at scale?
I've once again tried to come up with a clear and concise list for a vast amount of different answers.
Below are the 10 most promising methods.

| Method | Definition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep brain stimulation | Surgically placed electrodes stimulate deep targets directly, the only modality that reliably writes affective state today. | Implanted electrodes, closed-loop DBS |
| Cortical interfaces | Electrode arrays on or in cortex read neural activity at high resolution and can stimulate it, currently read-dominant. | Intracortical arrays, ECoG, endovascular stents |
| Transcranial magnetic stimulation | Magnetic pulses induce currents in superficial cortex, delivered in clinic across repeated sessions. | rTMS, theta-burst |
| Electrical stimulation | Weak scalp or nerve currents shift excitability at low cost from home, with highly variable response. | tDCS, tACS, taVNS |
| Focused ultrasound | Acoustic energy modulates deep targets non-invasively with millimeter precision, the only non-surgical route to subcortex. | Low-intensity transcranial FUS |
| Neuroplastogens | Compounds that open a plasticity window in which learned valuations can be durably rewritten. | Psilocybin, ketamine, MDMA, non-hallucinogenic analogs |
| Stimulants and nootropics | Monoamine and wake-promoting drugs that raise arousal and effort tolerance within an hour, limited by tolerance. | Amphetamine, methylphenidate, modafinil, caffeine |
| Neuroendocrine modulators | Endogenous hormones and peptides given exogenously to shift affective and motivational tone systemically. | Oxytocin, testosterone, cortisol, orexin agonists |
| Gene and molecular therapy | Engineered receptors or gene expression give cell-type-specific control of defined circuits, essentially permanent once delivered. | AAV delivery, chemogenetics, optogenetics |
| Behavioral training | Structured repeated practice that rewrites valuation through learning, cheap and safe but high effort across many sessions. | CBT, exposure, operant conditioning, neurofeedback |

All of these are currently being used or developed for programming personality in one way or another.
Behavioral training (ex: therapy) is the most clear example.
But everytime we have a cup of coffee (stimulant) to get work done, we attempt to get closer to our colleague that naturally has high levels of energy for their work, and thus a more desirable personality for this specific trait.
