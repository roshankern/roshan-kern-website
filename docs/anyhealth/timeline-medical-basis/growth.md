# Growth

Reference values for boys (the subject is male). Ages are in years; `0` means birth. "Adult" means the Snyder 1977 17–19.5 y bin, which is also the normalising row (factor 1.000). The code should always use `factor(age)`, which is `ratio(age) / ratio(adult)` taken from the **same** measurement. That factor is then applied to the rig's own adult segment. Do **not** divide a ratio from this file by a rig ratio: the landmarks differ, so ratios from different definitions don't mix. Same-measure factors assume that the offset between a surface landmark and the rig's joint centre scales in proportion to the segment, so it approximately cancels. That assumption is not measured here; expect a residual of perhaps a few %, largest in infants.

Rows the code cites (`// basis: growth#<key>`) carry two extra lines. **Code uses** states the table the code (`app/anyhealth/timeline/growth/proportions.ts`, `organs.ts`) actually uses, after the Task 14b reconciliation applied R1–R3, R6 and R7. **Agreement** compares it with the researched values. Recommendations are in the **Reconciliation** section at the end.

**How the segment tables were built.** Ages 2–18 use my medians of the Snyder 1977 individual male records (NIST AnthroKids `individuals.csv`, 1,978 boys; n per cell is in the detail tables). The file holds no infants. Ages 0–1 come from the Snyder 1975 male summary tables, which include infants from 0–3 months. Those means are linearly interpolated at the bin mid-ages (0–3 mo taken as 1.5 mo, 4–6 mo as 5 mo, 7–9 mo as 8 mo, and so on). A 1975 proxy measure is chained onto the 1977 joint-landmark measure at an anchor age (3 y for limb segments, 2 y for the foot and girths), so `value(age) = L77(anchor) × P75(age) / P75(anchor)`. The `0` row is the 0–3 month bin (about 1.5 months old), held flat back to birth. Segment ratios change by less than 2% over those six weeks.

## Reconciliation (research vs Task 5 code)

These were recommendations for the orchestrator. **Status (Task 14b):** R1, R2, R3, R6 and R7 are applied in the code as written, with two stated choices: the head keeps k = 0 (soft girth = head bone girth, as R1 implies), and the ICRP adult prostate and epididymis masses are placed at 20 y like the thymus and liver. R4 and R5 needed no change. Every factor below is relative to the 18 y / adult value of the same measure.

**R1. Soft-girth base (change recommended).** The code sets `softGirth = boneGirth × BMI term`. The Snyder 1977 data show that soft-tissue circumference does not scale like bone breadth in children. At median BMI the upper-arm circumference factor is 1.07 at 2 y, 0.90 at 6 y and 0.87 at 10 y, while the code's wrist-based soft girth is 1.249, 1.106 and 1.012 (13–23% too thick at 2–12 y). The thigh is 17–22% too thick at 0–8 y. Recommended: `softGirth[s] = girthCirc[s](age) × clamp(1 + 0.5·(BMI − BMI_ref)/BMI_ref, 0.85, 1.25)`, with `girthCirc` from the `girth-circ` row. Map upper arm → upper-arm circumference, forearm and hand → forearm, thigh → thigh, shank and foot → calf, trunk → mean of chest and waist, neck → neck (held at its 2 y value 1.31 below 2 y), head → head-breadth bone factor, since head circumference has k = 0. Equivalently, keep the code's form and multiply by `M(age)` from the `girth-soft` row. Keep k = 0.5 and the code's BMI_REF, both of which agree.

**R2. Testis (change recommended; adult age now agrees).** The code reaches adult size at 18 y, which matches the data (16 y ≈ 82% of adult volume, 18 y ≈ 98%). Its curve shape mixes methods, though. Recommended `TESTIS` nodes, ultrasound P50 in mL (Joustra 2015): 0: 0.23; 0.5: 0.40; 2: 0.43; 4: 0.46; 6: 0.53; 8: 0.58; 10: 0.75; 11: 1.0; 12: 2.0; 13: 3.9; 14: 6.5; 15: 8.8; 16: 10.8; 17: 12.1; 18: 12.9; 19: 13.1. This changes the linear factor by −29% at birth and −17% at 12–13 y relative to the current code. For the prostate and epididymides use their ICRP mass curves (`prostate`: 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.6, 4.3, 17 g at 0, 1, 5, 10, 15 y and adult; `epididymis`: 0.25, 0.35, 0.45, 0.6, 1.6, 4 g). Seminal vesicles have no reference, so let them follow the prostate.

**R3. Neck, infant factor (keep, with one added node).** The code's 3–18 y neck (menton→suprasternale, 0.054–0.064) is the same Snyder data as mine and should stay. My earlier "flat 1.0" used a different proxy (tragion→suprasternale), which only makes sense paired with a tragion-based head. Since the code uses the chin-based head (R5), the chin-based neck is the consistent partner. Below 3 y, the code's 0.030 at birth is an uncited assumption. It is, however, consistent with the one derived constraint, vertex→suprasternale ≈ 0.27–0.275 of stature at birth (code head + neck = 0.2677, within 3%). Recommended: keep 0.030 at 0 y, labelled as an assumption, and add a 2 y node of 0.050 (Snyder 1977 measured: vertex→suprasternale 0.2391 − chin→vertex 0.1891, n=6). Currently the code interpolates 0.0499 there, so this is effectively a no-op that makes the basis explicit.

**R4. Same-measure factors against rig-anchored ratios (resolved).** `proportions.ts` computes `length[s] = LEN[s](age) / LEN[s](18)`, a same-measure factor, and never divides a reference ratio by a rig ratio. This is what the file recommends. Organ factors normalise to the body at the last measurement (≈22.5 y), which is also consistent. No action.

**R5. Head measure (keep the code's chin→vertex).** The rig head segment carries the whole head, jaw included, and the warp scales it by one along-axis factor about the AO joint. So the visible vertex→chin height scales by exactly that factor. Chin→vertex (factor 1.894 at birth, 1.507 at 2 y, 1.291 at 6 y) therefore gives the correct visible head height. Tragion→vertex (2.18, 1.72, 1.40) would make the infant head about 15% too tall overall, even though it better matches cranial vault height. One factor cannot capture the infant's small face under a large cranium; the chin→vertex choice keeps the silhouette right. The code's head girth (head breadth, 2.21 at birth) agrees with mine within 4%. No action.

**R6. Limb bone girth before 3 y (change recommended, only together with R1).** The code holds wrist and ankle breadth flat below 3 y (factor 1.25). The data rise towards infancy: 1977 at 2 y gives wrist 1.40 and ankle 1.39 (n=8), and chained on 1975 hand breadth the factors are 1.48 at 1 y, 1.53 at 0.5 y and 1.60 at birth (±10%). Recommended extra nodes, breadth ÷ stature: `W_WRIST` 0: 0.0395; 0.5: 0.0378; 1: 0.0366; 2: 0.0344. `W_ANKLE` 0: 0.0546; 0.5: 0.0523; 1: 0.0506; 2: 0.0476. - *Thymus:* replace `THYMUS` with the ICRP masses from the `thymus` row: 13, 30, 30, 40, 35, 25 g at 0, 1, 5, 10, 15 y and adult, with adult placed at 20–25 y. The current curve is 16% too small (linear) at 1 y and 6% at 4 y.
